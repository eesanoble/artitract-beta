# Artitract — beta

A guess-the-origin game. You are shown a photograph of a cultural artifact and
you answer two questions at once: **where was it made, and when.** One pin on a
map, one year on a timeline, locked in together.

Each half is scored out of 100 and they add, so a round is out of 200 and a set
of five is out of 1,000. Full marks anywhere inside the target; outside it,
credit decays with distance — to the nearest edge of the region for place, to
the nearer end of the date range for time.

The question is not "where was this made" but **"what do the experts say?"** The
game asks whether you can find what the catalogue records. The varying size of
the target is the catalogue's confidence, made visible.

**This is a beta.** Five sets, all open at once, play them in any order. The real
thing puts out one set a day at midnight UTC.

## Rights

Artifacts are from [The Metropolitan Museum of Art's Open Access
collection](https://www.metmuseum.org/hubs/open-access), used under CC0. **The Met
is a data source, not a partner, and has not endorsed this.** CC0 covers the
images and the data; it does not cover the Met's trademarks.

Boundaries from [Natural Earth](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/) (public
domain), used as target zones and never as a claim about sovereignty.
